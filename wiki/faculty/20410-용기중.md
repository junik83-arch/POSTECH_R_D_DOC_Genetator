Overall water splitting with nanomaterials catalyst
Water splitting is a broad term referring to a chemical process where water is separated into oxygen and hydrogen. Efficient and cost-effective water splitting is crucial for the development of a hydrogen economy. Electrochemical water splitting, in particular, is recognized as the most promising technology for green hydrogen production. We are synthesizing nanomaterial electrode catalysts suitable for use in water electrolysis systems, employing various transition metals. Different synthesis methods, such as hydrothermal, thermal evaporation, chemical vapor deposition (CVD), and electrodeposition, are utilized to create transition metal compounds with diverse compositions, including hydroxides, phosphides, and sulfides.
Furthermore, we have successfully integrated photovoltaic (PV) cells to establish self-powered electrochemical systems. A series-connected PV cell provides sufficient electrical power to drive electrochemical reactions. We have also developed a self-powered water electrolysis system that operates solely on solar energy by coupling the PV cell with the electrolysis cell, allowing for solar-driven water splitting.

Hybrid water splitting
Overall water splitting involves the hydrogen evolution reaction (HER) for hydrogen production and the oxygen evolution reaction (OER) for oxygen generation. Given its 4-electron process, the OER exhibits slow reactivity and demands relatively high overpotentials. To address these issues, we explore hybrid water splitting that substitute the OER with reactions like urea oxidation or hydrazine oxidation, which have lower theoretical potentials. These alternatives aim to enhance the efficiency of water splitting by addressing the sluggish kinetics and high overpotential associated with the OER.
