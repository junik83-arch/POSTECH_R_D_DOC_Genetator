원자력공학은 원자력 에너지의 생성과 활용 전반을 다루는 종합 학문으로, 핵연료 개발에서부터 원자로의 설계·운영, 더 나아가 사용후핵연료 및 방사성 폐기물의 안전한 관리와 처리까지 폭넓은 연구와 이해를 필요로 합니다. 최근 고효율·친환경 에너지 수요가 급격히 증가함에 따라 원자력의 역할이 전 세계적으로 재조명되고 있으며, 이른바 ‘제2의 원자력 르네상스’라 불리는 시기를 맞이하고 있습니다. 앞으로 원자력공학은 단순한 에너지 생산을 넘어 안전한 에너지 관리와 방사성 폐기물의 처리·처분 등 지속 가능한 안전성 확보에 더욱 초점을 맞추게 될 것입니다.
2011년 일본 후쿠시마 원자력 발전소 사고는 원자력의 지속적이고 안전한 이용을 위해 방사성 폐기물, 특히 사용후핵연료의 철저한 관리가 필수적임을 여실히 보여주었습니다. 이어 2025년 3월 「고준위 방사성폐기물 관리에 관한 특별법」이 국회를 통과함으로써, 40여 년간 표류하던 고준위 폐기물 처분 정책이 처음으로 법적·제도적 기반을 갖추게 되었습니다. 이는 우리나라가 국가 차원에서 중·장기적인 폐기물 관리 및 처분 연구를 본격적으로 추진할 수 있는 전환점이자, 안전하고 지속 가능한 원자력 이용을 위한 중요한 이정표라 할 수 있습니다.

Our Research Interest & Field of Specialization
Our lab advances both interdisciplinary and specialized research across key areas of the nuclear fuel cycle's back end—including decontamination, immobilization, geological disposal, and environmental remediation—to contribute to the long-term sustainability of nuclear energy.
Decontamination
Our research focuses on the decontamination of diverse radioactive wastes generated from nuclear power plants. We develop and optimize chemical and physical treatment methods tailored to various waste matrices, such as metallic components, concrete debris, and secondary wastes. By improving decontamination efficiency and minimizing secondary waste generation, we contribute to safer and more cost-effective nuclear waste management.

Immobilization
We study the immobilization of radionuclides in stable waste forms to ensure their long-term isolation from the environment. Our work focuses on the development and performance evaluation of various matrices, including glass, geopolymer, ceramic and cement-based materials. We investigate their chemical stability, radiation resistance, and durability under conditions relevant to geological disposal.

Atomic level investigation of bentonite change under extreme environments (2025-2028)
: Impact on the structural stability and radionuclide immobilization capacity of engineering barrier in high-level radioactive waste repository || In a high-level radioactive waste (HLW) repository, the concrete lining surrounding the bentonite buffer can release strongly alkaline solutions due to the combined effects of groundwater infiltration and radiant heat generated by radioactive waste decay. When these highly alkaline solutions come into contact with bentonite, the chemical properties of the bentonite can be altered, which in turn could be significant for the safety of the HLW repository. Therefore, it is essential to investigate the combined influence of chemical (high alkalinity) and physical (elevated temperature) conditions on atomic-level changes in bentonite structure and their effects on the sorption and immobilization behavior of Cesium radionuclides (137Cs) in HLW repository to assess the long-term safety of radioactive waste disposal. The major objectives of this research project are the detailed investigation of the atomic level changes in the frameworks/structure of bentonite clays (MX-80 bentonite (Na+-bentonite) from Wyoming and WRK bentonite (Ca2+-bentonite) from Mongolia used in South Korean repository) due to their treatment under (extreme) environmental (physicochemical) conditions. After physicochemical treatments, the generated void and chemical reactivity changes in the structure of bentonite clays can be significant to alter the retention capacity and thereby, the immobilization behavior of 137Cs in repository over a longer period.

New insights on the immobilization of radioactive acidic liquid waste and cesium in amorphous aluminophosphate waste form (2026)
Cement and Concrete Research, Volume 203, May 2026, 108169. Abstract: Radioactive acidic liquid waste (RALW), generated during the Chemical Oxidation Reduction Decontamination (CORD) and High Permanganate CORD Ultraviolet (HP-CORD UV) process of primary systems in nuclear power plants, contains various heavy metals and various radionuclides. These are typically removed by organic ion exchange resins, producing large amounts of secondary spent ion exchange resins (spent IERs) waste. This study aimed to reduce spent IER generation by directly using unpurified RALW as mixing solution for amorphous aluminophosphate (AAP) waste forms which use acidic solution as a raw material. In addition, Cs that may be present in RALW was added at 10 wt% to investigate its effect on the AAP structure. Neither the use of RALW as mixing solution nor the addition of Cs significantly altered the mechanical strength of AAP waste forms. The presence of heavy metal in RALW did not substantially change the local structure of Al and P in the AAP matrix. Multiscale characterization showed that Cs was mainly immobilized by forming amorphous cesium aluminophosphate and through physical incorporation within the AAP matrix. This research demonstrates that RALW can be effectively used in AAP waste forms and provides direct evidence of Cs immobilization mechanisms in AAP systems.
