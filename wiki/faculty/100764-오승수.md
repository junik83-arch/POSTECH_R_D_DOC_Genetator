Synergy of Science with Engineering
​
Science
and
engineering
are not separate but interact with each other. Intriguing scientific questions trigger methodological advances, and the advancement in technology stimulates another scientific curiosity. Within this reciprocal interaction,
Materials Science and Engineering has advanced and integrated them based on many novel materials.
Our lab is strongly interested in creating
novel
macr
o- and biomolecular materials that perform "protein-like" or "cell-like" molecular functions
, and we also develop
innovative techniques in addressing life-related fundamental questions
.
Pioneering Proteomimetics and Cytomimetics
To maintain a life, biological cells have incredible capabilities--
1)
replication/transmission/replacement of genetic information,
2)
catalyzing enzymatic reactions,
3)
recognition of molecular targets, and
4)
even regulation of molecule transport into and out of the cells. When looking into this amazing world, it is discovered that “
sequence-defined biopolymers
”, including nucleic acids (DNAs and RNAs), amino acids (proteins), and saccharides (carbohydrates), are the core elements of the living cells. Depending on the specific sequence of the biopolymers, they fold into unique 3D nanostructures that control a cellular life, and this is one of the essential principles in MOLECULAR BIOLOGY. Based on the fundamental principle, we intend to create highly ‘sequence-controlled’ biopolymers capable of performing valuable molecular functions, using innovative technologies: rational design and Darwinian evolution. With the newly developed molecular materials, we challenge not only the traditional fields of biotechnology such as the development of
high-performance biosensors
and
highly effective therapeutic agents
, but also creative and innovative ventures such as implementing
artificial cells
via sophisticated control of cellular functions, for which emerging SYNTHETIC BIOLOGY is aiming.
