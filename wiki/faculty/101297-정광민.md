> 정광민 교수는 AIRM Lab 소속으로, 보험 계리 모델링, 보험 및 리스크 관리 연구를 수행하고 있습니다. 주요 연구 분야는 보험 맥락에서의 사이버 리스크, 보험 산업의 인슈어테크 및 디지털화, 재난(극한) 리스크 모델링, 신흥 리스크 분석, 의존성 모델링 및 리스크 통합 등입니다. 정 교수는 2026 MRS 국제 리스크 컨퍼런스에서 "중소기업의 이질적인 보험 수요 체제: 경영자 선호도 및 재정 제약의 역할"을 발표했으며, 제10회 아시아 양적 금융 컨퍼런스에서 "사이버 리스크 전염이 가격에 반영되는가? 주식 수익률의 횡단면 증거"에 대해 초청 강연을 했습니다. 또한, North American Actuarial Journal의 우수 심사위원, Journal of Risk and Insurance의 2022-2023년 최다 인용 논문 저자, 한국화재보험협회 공로패 수상 등 다수의 수상 경력이 있습니다.

Cyber risk in actuarial / insurance context
Cyber risk is one of the risks that companies should be aware of. Not only can a cyber attack result in the loss of assets, but other costs such as compensation or lawsuits can also threaten the business.
Insurance can mitigate these risks through risk distribution or mutual aid. Our research into these risks includes the following:
Cyber loss prediction
Measurement of financial impact
Analysis of extreme cyber loss events
InsurTech / Digitalization in insurance
Digitalization and InsurTech are reshaping the insurance ecosystem, affecting distribution channels, customer experience, and operational efficiency.
These changes introduce both opportunities and risks, requiring new actuarial and risk management approaches. Our research into these topics includes the following:
Machine learning techniques in actuarial modeling
Study on business disruption by InsurTech
Catastrophe (extreme) risk modeling
Estimation of loss caused by extreme events is important to prevent bankruptcy. However, it is challenging due to not only lack of historical data but also continously changed system causing the events.
Our research into these risks includes the following:
Natural disaster reduction
Man-made catastrophe management
Probable maximum loss estimation
Emerging risk analysis
Insurance companies measure risk based on historical data. However, some risks, such as climate change and cyber risk, have only recently emerged, so there is no related data. These risks are called emerging risks. Our research into these risks includes the following:
Loss modelling of emerging risks
Insurance customer behaviour in the pandemic era
Digital risks as operational risks
Dependence modeling & Risk aggregation
Many statistical analyses assume that the samples are independent. However, some risks, such as the spread of infectious diseases, affect other samples, and these kinds of risk are called systemic risks.
Understanding the structures of dependency is important for correctly estimating losses. Our research into these risks includes the following:
Understanding interdependency in risks (heterogeneous risks)
Copula approaches in actuarial modelling
Risk aggregation in insurance

Systemic cyber risks and insurance regulatory capital (forthcoming)
Systemic cyber risk can cascade across interconnected networks, generating substantial Incurred But Not Reported (IBNR) losses that threaten insurers' capital adequacy. We present a framework that embeds IBNR into Solvency Capital Requirement (SCR calculations by coupling an SIR epidemic model parameterized by firms' operational resilience and sectoral network structure—with Monte Carlo simulations of loss propagation. Our analysis shows that highly resilient firms recognize losses more quickly, leading to shorter reporting periods and consequently higher IBNR in the early stages, whereas low resilient firms need longer reporting periods, causing their losses to increase over time. Sectoral comparison reveals more severe systemic impacts in finance than in information and demonstrates that large insurers maintain adequate solvency buffers while smaller insurers face solvency pressures. These findings highlight the need to incorporate IBNR, resilience, and network topology in cyber-SCR modeling.
Keywoong Bae

Matrix-based Factor Analysis on the Prediction of Insurance Claims Probability
We propose a matrix-based factor analysis model for predicting the probability of insurance claims. The model employs projected principal component analysis (PPCA), which enhances the estimation of unobserved latent factors by projecting a data matrix onto a linear space spanned by insured-specific features. This approach addresses the overparameterization problem when the number of insured-specific features and insurance coverages is large, enabling more accurate estimation of claim probability than conventional methods. Using a large-scale health insurance dataset from a leading life insurer in South Korea, we demonstrate that the proposed model outperforms conventional and machine-learning benchmarks, such as logistic regression and XGBoost, in predicting claim probabilities. We further determine that our model can reduce computational time by approximately 86% and 98% compared to logistic regression and XGBoost, respectively. The proposed model provides a unified and scalable framework for modeling high-dimensional claim probabilities, offering practical value for underwriting, risk management, and personalized insurance product design.

Systemic Cyber Risk and the Equilibrium Equity Premium
This study examines the asset pricing implications of systemic cyber risk. We consider an insurer exposed to systemic cyber events that jointly affect insurance losses and financial market conditions in a regime-switching economy. The insurer transfers part of its cyber exposure to financial investors through a cyber-catastrophe bond, under which investors receive fixed coupon payments while optimally choosing consumption and portfolio allocations. Using a general equilibrium framework, we show that systemic cyber risk is priced through a covariance channel in the equilibrium equity premium. The resulting risk premium depends not only on aggregate dividend risk but crucially also on the covariance between systemic cyber events and market conditions.
