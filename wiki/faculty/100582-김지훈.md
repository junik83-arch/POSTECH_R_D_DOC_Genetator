Instrumentation
■ Cryogenic magnetic force microscopy platforms
Instrumentation development is a central part of the MFM Laboratory. We design and construct cryogenic magnetic force microscopy platforms that enable quantitative imaging of superconducting vortices, magnetic domains, and hybrid quantum materials under controlled temperature and magnetic-field conditions.
Our MFM systems combine low-temperature operation, high mechanical stability, sensitive force detection, and vector-field control. These capabilities allow the laboratory to go beyond conventional magnetic imaging and perform local, quantitative measurements of penetration depth, vortex pinning, vortex manipulation, and magnetic-domain evolution.
■ Cryogenic dual-scanner MFM with piezoresistive cantilever
We developed a cryogenic dual-scanner magnetic force microscope equipped with a piezoresistive cantilever. The piezoresistive detection scheme removes the need for optical alignment during temperature changes and provides a stable platform for low-temperature MFM measurements.
The dual-scanner design combines coarse and fine scanning capabilities, enabling flexible sample positioning and high-resolution local imaging. This system is particularly useful for comparative studies of superconducting vortices and magnetic textures over a wide temperature range.

■ Vector-field cryogenic MFM
We constructed a vector-field cryogenic magnetic force microscope to investigate magnetic and superconducting phenomena under independently controlled magnetic-field directions. Vector-field capability is essential for studying anisotropic superconductors, chiral magnetic textures, vortex configurations, and magnetic-domain evolution under tilted magnetic fields.
This platform enables three-dimensional control of the magnetic field while maintaining cryogenic MFM imaging capability, providing a powerful experimental tool for spatially resolved studies of superconductors, van der Waals magnets, and topological magnetic materials.
