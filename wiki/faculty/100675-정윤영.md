AI Speech Processing
AI Speech Enhancement Model for Smart Wearable Devices
In noisy environments such as factories, construction sites, or disaster scenes, real-time speech communication is challenging because acoustic microphones are highly susceptible to background noise.
Bone-conduction microphones (BCMs) offer strong noise robustness but lack high-frequency details, leading to muffled speech. By combining the noise robustness of BCMs with regular acoustic microphone signals, we develop a multimodal speech enhancement model that produces clear and natural speech. The model is further compressed through pruning and quantization, enabling efficient real-time deployment on mobile and wearable devices.

Next-generation Memory
Next-Generation Memory Devices
Conventional 1T1C DRAM faces significant scaling limitations due to the challenges in storage capacitor fabrication and low speed.
Our research focuses on capacitorless 2T DRAM, which utilizes oxide semiconductors with extremely low leakage current. This approach offers long data retention, reduces power consumption, and eliminates the need for a storage capacitor, enabling faster and more energy-efficient computing systems.
Synaptic Devices for In-Memory Computing
The exponential growth in artificial intelligence (AI) has significantly increased data movement between the processor and memory, resulting in the von Neumann bottleneck, which reduces system performance and increases power consumption. In-Memory Computing (IMC) addresses this issue by performing computations directly within the memory, minimizing data transfer.
We study novel cell/array structures and efficient parallel operation methodologies, utilizing oxide semiconductor-based memory devices to achieve low-power, highly accurate AI computations.
Reconfigurable In-memory Computing Architectures
AI models such as vision, language, and biosignals demand layer-specific computation and precision. Conventional GPUs, however, lack hardware-level layer-wise optimization due to their uniform computational framework. Edge devices like mobiles and autonomous vehicles further require hardware that ensures low power, low latency, and compact design.
We study reconfigurable In-memory computing architectures utilizing oxide-semiconductor synaptic arrays that enable efficient modulations by tuning charge-based weight characteristics. This capability supports layer-by-layer optimization and provides reconfigurable, scalable hardware toward general-purpose AI processors.
