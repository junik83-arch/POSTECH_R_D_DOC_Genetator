8
Open-Vocabulary Scene Understanding
Open-vocabulary scene understanding reflects a paradigm shift away from recognition systems built around a small, fixed set of predefined categories. Classical segmentation and detection models can only recognize the specific classes they were trained on — a "chair" model has no way to name or localize a "stool" if that word was never in its label set. Open-vocabulary perception instead couples visual recognition with language, letting a model segment, ground, and reason about an arbitrary object category described in natural language at test time, whether or not that category ever appeared in training.
7
Active Perception
Active perception is grounded in a paradigm shift that computer vision underwent as it moved beyond passive, all-at-once scene analysis. Rather than treating a camera or a model as a fixed device that must take in an entire scene and try to make sense of everything it sees, active perception treats the observer as something that can choose — where to look, how to move through and interact with a scene, or which view to prioritize — in order to answer a specific question as efficiently as possible.
6
Embodied AI & Language-Driven Robot Manipulation
The lab's embodied AI line investigates why end-to-end vision-language-action (VLA) models struggle with long-horizon manipulation, and proposes a modular approach centered on open-vocabulary affordance grounding rather than mapping instructions directly to low-level actions. The core hypothesis is that manipulation becomes tractable when it is decomposed into intermediate, physically grounded representations — where on an object a task-relevant region is, how a hand should contact it, and how the whole body should move to get there — rather than learned as one opaque instruction-to-action mapping. This is a deliberate move away from black-box, end-to-end learning toward a more analytical and explicit formulation of manipulation, where each intermediate representation can be inspected, interpreted, and reasoned about on its own terms.
